About the survey

The Socio-Economic Insights Survey (SEIS) 2026 was implemented by REACH Initiative with UNHCR in Moldova. It provides updated evidence on the needs, vulnerabilities, socio-economic conditions, integration gaps and self-reliance of refugee households from Ukraine, and builds on the 2024 and 2025 rounds. Methodological and questionnaire changes limit direct comparison over time.

Data were collected nationwide between 16 September and 9 October 2026 by computer-assisted telephone interviewing (CATI). Eligible households had at least one member with solely Ukrainian citizenship, displaced to Moldova since February 2022, who registered on the temporary protection platform for an extension (1–16 February or 1–30 April 2026) or for a first application (1 May–18 August 2026).

Households were selected by simple random sampling from a deduplicated frame of 30,788 households. In total, 1,291 phone numbers were contacted and 331 household interviews were retained, giving a 95% confidence level and a 5.5% margin of error. Data cover all household members (751), including some Moldovan citizens and people not displaced from Ukraine, so the population behind each indicator varies; education, language, health and work indicators refer to Ukrainian members.

Main limitations: findings represent the registration list only, not all refugees in Moldova; households without working phones are missed; income, expenses, health and other sensitive topics are self-reported and may be under- or over-reported; some questions may have been interpreted differently by respondents.

Key concepts

Labour force status follows the International Labour Organization (ILO) framework: employed, unemployed (without work, available and seeking or waiting to start) or outside the labour force. The potential labour force is the part of those outside who want work but are not seeking or not available. Adapted for refugees, people available for work who have stopped searching are counted as unemployed.
